01 — Abstract

About

I hold two master's degrees — one in Computer Engineering with a specialization in Data Science and Analytics, and one in Public Administration — an unusual pairing I chose deliberately. Technical systems increasingly make decisions that used to belong to institutions; I want to be able to build those systems and write the policy that governs them, not just one or the other.

My engineering work has covered NLP for low-resource languages, credit risk modelling, and medical image classification. My policy training pushes me to keep asking who a model serves, who it excludes, and what happens when it's wrong. I'm currently based in Kathmandu, working at Nepal Telecom, and preparing applications to funded, structured doctoral programs that sit at this same intersection.

Keywords — Natural Language Processing · Machine Learning · Credit Risk Modelling · Digital Governance · AI Regulation · Telecommunications Policy

02 — Areas of Inquiry

Research interests

NLP

Low-resource language models

Adapting transformer architectures to Nepali and other underrepresented languages, where training data is scarce and evaluation benchmarks are still being built.

ML

Applied predictive modelling

Boosting and ensemble methods for credit risk and default prediction, with attention to class imbalance and what a false negative actually costs someone.

POLICY

Digital & telecom governance

How regulators in emerging markets can keep pace with AI and platform infrastructure — drawing on my day-to-day work inside a national telecom operator.

VISION

Medical image classification

Earlier work applying CNN architectures (AlexNet, VGG-16) to diagnostic imaging — malaria and blood cancer detection — where I first saw model error translate directly into human stakes.
